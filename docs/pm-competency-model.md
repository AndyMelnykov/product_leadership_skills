# PM Competency Model

This document explains the product management competency model used in this repository. It is based on the PM competency model by Ravi Mehta.

## Why the model matters

The model helps people grow and align expectations across the organization. It creates a shared language for:

- coaching and development
- performance review and calibration
- hiring and interview evaluation

The idea is to make competencies visible, observable, and consistent. Once teams agree on what good looks like, it becomes much easier to support growth, evaluate performance, and hire candidates who can succeed in the role.

## How the model connects to performance and hiring

The model works best when used across the full employee lifecycle:

- competencies define expected behavior and outcomes
- performance reviews assess evidence of those competencies
- interview questions are designed to evaluate the same competencies

This alignment matters because if hiring interviews evaluate different signals than performance reviews, the organization creates confusion. Great hiring practices should reflect the actual work the role requires, and performance review standards should reflect the same underlying capabilities.

## The four competency categories

### 1. Product Execution

This is the foundation of product work.

- Feature Specification
- Product Delivery
- Product Quality

### 2. Customer Insight

This focuses on understanding customers and using evidence to make decisions.

- Fluency with Data
- Voice of the Customer
- User Experience Design

### 3. Product Strategy

This aligns product work with business outcomes and long-term direction.

- Business Outcome Ownership
- Product Vision & Roadmapping
- Strategic Impact

### 4. Influencing People

This ensures product leaders can align teams and stakeholders.

- Stakeholder Management
- Team Leadership
- Managing Up

## Model use guidance

The competency model should be used as a practical tool for:

- evaluating strengths and gaps
- discussing development opportunities
- setting expectations for role progression
- guiding interview rubrics and calibration sessions

When used well, the model reduces bias, improves consistency, and gives product leaders a clearer way to support both performance and growth.

## Important principle

A competency model is most valuable when it is used consistently. If a manager uses one standard in performance reviews and a different standard in hiring, the result is misalignment. The recommended approach is to ensure that interview questions, evaluation rubrics, and development discussions all map back to the same competency framework.
