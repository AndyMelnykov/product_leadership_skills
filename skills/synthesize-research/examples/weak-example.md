# Weak Example: Onboarding Confusion Synthesis (same input as the strong example)

## Key Findings

**Finding 1: Onboarding is confusing**

Users find onboarding confusing. The average satisfaction score dropped after the new onboarding flow launched.

This is a high-confidence finding based on customer interviews.

## Recommendation

Redesign the onboarding flow immediately to fix the confusion.

## Why this is weak

- **Reports an average with no distribution.** "Satisfaction score dropped" cites an aggregate without showing the range — it's impossible to tell if this is a broad problem or driven by a handful of outliers.
- **Claims high confidence with no participant count given.** "Customer interviews" could mean 2 or 20 — a reader can't judge whether this is a hypothesis or a well-supported conclusion, and the synthesis presents it as settled either way.
- **No source attribution.** No quotes, no segment breakdown, no way to trace the claim back to actual evidence.
- **Ignores the contradicting survey data entirely.** The 200-response NPS write-ins showing no onboarding complaints are never mentioned, so the reader can't judge whether this finding generalizes or is scoped to one segment.
- **Jumps straight to a recommendation** ("redesign immediately") without surfacing the open question of which user segment is actually affected, or proposing a way to resolve the contradiction first.
